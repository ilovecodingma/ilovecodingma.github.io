Claude for Small Product Businesses: Labels, Margins, Reviews

Published 2026-10-19

This guide is for people who run a small product business — a sauce brand, a candle shop, a handmade cosmetics line — and want to use Claude for the repetitive paperwork around the product, not for the product itself. Every workflow below runs in a normal chat window in under 15 minutes, with no code.

1What Claude is good at here (and what it is not)

TaskGood fit?Why
Checking label copy for consistency and missing itemsYesIt compares text against a checklist you give it, line by line.
Drafting a wholesale pitch and a margin tableYesArithmetic on numbers you supply, plus formatting.
Summarising competitor reviewsYesGrouping hundreds of short texts into themes is a core strength.
Deciding whether a health or safety claim is legalNoThat is your regulator's call. Use Claude to list questions, then check the official rules.
Inventing prices, costs or market sizesNoIt will produce plausible-looking numbers. Only give it numbers you know.

2Workflow 1 — Label copy check

  1. Paste the full label text (front and back) and the product's ingredient list.
  2. Paste the official labeling checklist for your market. In the US that is the FDA food labeling pages; in Korea, the MFDS labeling standards.
  3. Ask: Compare the label to the checklist. Output a table: requirement, found on label (quote), missing or unclear. Do not judge legality.
  4. Fix what is missing, then run the same prompt on the new label to confirm nothing regressed.

The value is the quote column: every "found" row must point to real text on your label, so you can verify it in seconds.

3Workflow 2 — Wholesale proposal and margin table

  1. Write down your real numbers: unit cost, packaging, shipping per case, case size, retail price.
  2. Ask for a table with wholesale price at several discount levels off retail, your margin per unit and per case, and the retailer's margin at their usual markup.
  3. Ask it to show the formula for each column. Recalculate one row yourself; if it matches, the rest is consistent.
  4. Then ask for a one-page pitch that cites only the numbers in the table.

4Workflow 3 — Competitor listing review analysis

  1. Copy public reviews from two or three competing listings into a text file, keeping the star rating with each review.
  2. Ask: Group these reviews into complaint themes and praise themes. For each theme: count, two short quotes, and which star ratings it appears in.
  3. Turn the top complaint themes into a checklist for your own listing: photos, size info, shipping expectations, ingredients.

Always ask for counts and quotes. A theme without quotes is a guess.

5Workflow 4 — The 20-minute weekly check

  • Paste last week's order summary, refunds and customer messages (names and addresses removed).
  • Ask for three things that changed vs. the previous week (with the numbers), any repeated customer question, and one listing or FAQ change that would answer it.
  • Keep each week's answer in one document. After a month, ask Claude to compare the four summaries.

6FAQ

Can I trust the arithmetic?
Check one row by hand. If the formula shown is right and one row matches, the table is consistent; if not, ask it to recompute and show its work.
Should I upload customer personal data?
Remove names, emails and addresses first. None of these workflows need them.
Which plan or model do I need?
All four workflows are short chat tasks. Start with the plan you already have; current options are on Anthropic's pricing page.
